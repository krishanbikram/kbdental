Tooth Filling & Cavity Repair in Kathmandu Valley

Halt active dental decay, eliminate localized sensitivity, and restore natural tooth contours with aesthetic, tooth-colored composite restorations.

What is a Tooth Filling? Direct Answer

A tooth filling is a conservative restorative dental procedure that repairs tooth damage caused by bacterial decay (dental caries) or mechanical chipping. The dentist gently removes demineralized enamel and infected dentin, thoroughly cleans the cavity preparation, and fills the void with a biocompatible material to restore normal biting function and prevent bacteria from entering deeper pulp tissue. Modern clinics coordinated by KB Dental across Kathmandu Valley utilize high-strength composite resin that bonds micromechanically to natural enamel, matching your tooth's exact shade so the restoration is virtually invisible.

Signs You Have a Cavity Requiring a Filling

Dental enamel has no living nerve endings; therefore, early cavities rarely cause noticeable pain. However, once decay penetrates the outer enamel into the softer underlying dentin, warning symptoms emerge. You should consult an experienced dentist in Kathmandu if you experience:

  • Mild Sensitivity to Temperature: Brief, momentary twinges when drinking cold water or hot tea that subside almost immediately after swallowing.
  • Sweet Sensitivity: Discomfort when eating chocolates, mithai, or sugary pastries, caused by osmotic fluid shifts through exposed dentinal tubules.
  • Food Trapping (Food Impaction): Shredding of dental floss or stubborn food debris lodging between adjacent teeth after every meal.
  • Visible Discoloration or Dark Pit: A grey, brown, or black spot visible on the biting fissures of molars or between anterior teeth.
  • Sharp or Jagged Tooth Edges: A chip or hole you can feel with your tongue.

Restorative Materials: Composite vs GIC vs Amalgam

When receiving care through a local Kathmandu dentist, your provider will select the restorative material best suited to the cavity's location and depth:

1. Tooth-Colored Composite Resin

The modern gold standard. Composed of ceramic-filled dimethacrylate polymer that bonds directly to tooth structure. Hardens in seconds under blue LED light, allowing immediate eating and flawless cosmetic blending.

2. Glass Ionomer Cement (GIC)

A self-adhesive dental cement that releases fluoride ions over time to protect against recurrent caries. Frequently utilized for cervical root cavities, pediatric baby teeth, and deep base linings.

3. Silver Amalgam (Traditional)

The older metallic alloy. Extremely long-lasting on posterior molars, but requires excessive healthy tooth reduction to create mechanical retention and presents an unnatural dark silver appearance.

4. Ceramic Inlays & Onlays

Laboratory-milled porcelain restorations for massive cavities where a standard filling is inadequate, but sufficient tooth remains to avoid a full dental crown.

Step-by-Step Tooth Filling Procedure

  1. Examination & Local Anesthesia (If Needed): For shallow enamel decay, no anesthesia is needed. For deeper cavities near dentin, localized numbing ensures complete comfort throughout.
  2. Gentle Decay Removal: High-precision micro-burs remove soft bacteria and demineralized tooth structure while preserving healthy enamel.
  3. Acid Etching & Adhesive Bonding: A mild phosphoric acid gel etches microscopic porosities into enamel. A medical dental bonding agent is painted on and cured to create an ultra-strong chemical bond.
  4. Incremental Composite Placement & Light Curing: Shade-matched composite resin is applied in thin 2mm increments. A high-intensity blue LED light (450nm) hardens each layer in 20 seconds.
  5. Bite Adjustment & High-Gloss Polish: Articulating paper verifies that your bite alignment is completely natural. The filling is polished with fine finishing discs to create a smooth, plaque-resistant surface.

When is a Filling No Longer Sufficient?

If a cavity is neglected for months, bacteria eventually penetrate through the dentin into the vascular pulp chamber. Once the nerve becomes infected, a filling is no longer capable of resolving the problem, as sealing bacteria inside will cause a painful dental abscess. In these circumstances, the tooth requires a root canal treatment (RCT) to remove the infection from the root canals, followed by a protective dental crown. Treating cavities early with conservative fillings saves you time, discomfort, and significant expense!
