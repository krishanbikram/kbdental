What is a Dental Crown (Cap)? Direct Answer

A dental crown, commonly known as a tooth cap, is a custom-fabricated prosthetic restoration that completely encases the visible portion of a compromised tooth down to the gumline. Crowns serve as protective armor for teeth that have undergone root canal treatment (RCT), teeth with extensive structural decay that cannot support a simple tooth filling, or teeth cracked from biting hard foods. Fabricated using high-strength monolithic zirconia, IPS e.max lithium disilicate, or porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM), a crown restored by an experienced dentist in Kathmandu re-establishes natural bite alignment, chewing strength, and realistic smile aesthetics for 10 to 20+ years.

When is a Dental Crown Necessary?

Dental caps are prescribed when a tooth's structural integrity has been critically diminished. The most common situations include:

  • Following Root Canal Therapy: Because an RCT tooth no longer has an internal blood supply, its dentin becomes dehydrated and brittle. A full-coverage cap prevents vertical root splits under heavy chewing forces.
  • Extensive Decay & Cuspal Fractures: When a cavity destroys more than 50% of the natural tooth crown, a conventional filling lacks sufficient surface area to survive bite pressures.
  • Cracked Tooth Syndrome: Teeth exhibiting hairline fractures that cause sharp pain upon releasing bite pressure. A crown binds the fractured segments together to halt crack propagation.
  • Completing a Dental Implant: After titanium osseointegration, a custom prosthetic crown is screwed or cemented onto the abutment to replace the missing tooth permanently, as detailed on our dental implants guide.
  • Severe Enamel Erosion or Cosmetic Defect: Severe fluorosis, intrinsic tetracycline staining, or malformed peg-shaped lateral incisors.

Crown Materials Compared: What is Best for You?

When consulting with a local Kathmandu dentist, you will choose from several material options based on tooth location and functional requirements:

1. Monolithic Zirconia

Milled from a solid block of zirconium dioxide using CAD/CAM software. Boasting a flexural strength of 1100–1200 MPa, it is virtually chip-proof, making it the premier choice for back molars where chewing forces are greatest.

2. IPS e.max (All-Ceramic)

High-translucency lithium disilicate glass-ceramic. Delivers the absolute highest aesthetic realism with lifelike light transmission, making it ideal for front incisors and smile makeovers.

3. Layered Zirconia

Features a high-strength zirconia core layered with hand-applied feldspathic porcelain on the facial surface, combining exceptional molar strength with natural aesthetic depth.

4. Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal (PFM)

The classic, economical standard. Combines a biocompatible metal alloy framework overlaid with tooth-colored porcelain. Highly dependable, though metal margins may become visible over time if gums recede.

The 2-Appointment Dental Crown Procedure

  1. Tooth Preparation & Core Build-Up: The dentist gently trims approximately 1.0 to 1.5 mm of outer enamel to create a tapered margin (chamfer or shoulder) so the crown fits flush with adjacent teeth. If tooth structure is deficient, a composite core build-up is bonded.
  2. Digital Scan / Silicone Impression: An accurate 3D intraoral optical scan or polyvinyl siloxane impression captures the prepared margin and opposing bite relationship. A temporary acrylic crown is placed to protect the tooth.
  3. Custom CAD/CAM Lab Milling: Specialized dental laboratories in Kathmandu design and mill your custom crown using robotic 5-axis milling machines, followed by high-temperature furnace sintering and color glazing.
  4. Try-in & Permanent Cementation: At your second appointment (usually 3 to 5 days later), the dentist verifies margin seal, bite occlusion, and color match before bonding the crown with high-adhesion resin cement.
